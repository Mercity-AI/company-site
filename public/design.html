Deciding what this place looks like before deciding what it says.

Everything on this page is a proposal. Three hues chosen off the wheel rather than picked by eye, the ramps built from them, five type pairings set in the same specimen so they can be judged against each other, a live calibration panel for the size-weight-contrast question, and four grain-washed surface treatments. Argue with any of it — nothing here has shipped.

01 — Colour

Three hues, chosen off the wheel rather than by eye.

The anchor is the indigo already in the stylesheet — #4f46e5, which is hsl(243 75% 59%). Rather than invent around it, the other two are derived from it geometrically. Its direct complement sits at 63°, an unusable olive; the split-complements either side of that are 33° and 93°. 33° is amber and reads as warmth and warning. 93° is chartreuse and reads as nothing good. So amber takes the signal slot, and the third is pulled in the other direction to 178° — teal, close enough to indigo to feel related, far enough to separate cleanly in a chart.

Indigo · 243° · anchor
Carries identity. Links, primary actions, the one word in a headline that earns emphasis. It is the only colour allowed to appear without a reason — everything else has a job.
Teal · 178° · instrument
65° from the anchor: related, not analogous. This is the data colour — benchmark bars, diagram strokes, code accents, anything that means measurement. It never competes with indigo for a call to action.
Amber · 34° · signal
The split-complement, 151° off the anchor. Reserved: a live badge, a caveat, a number that got worse. If amber shows up twice on one screen, one of the two is decoration and should be removed.
Neutral · 240° at 7–14% sat
Not grey. The whole neutral ramp is pulled toward the anchor hue, so text and rules sit in the same world as the accent instead of reading as a stock stylesheet. At these saturations you cannot name the tint, but you can tell when it is missing.
02 — Ramps

Ten steps each, built not sampled.

Every step holds the hue and moves saturation and lightness on a fixed curve, so the four ramps stay siblings. Contrast is measured against white and against ink 900 — click any swatch to copy its value.

03 — Combinations

Which pairs actually survive contact.

Contrast ratios computed live. AAA is 7.0, AA is 4.5, and anything under 3.0 is decoration only — never body text. The failures are left in deliberately: they are the combinations that look best in a swatch grid and collapse the moment you set 15px type in them.

05 — Calibration

Your hunch, made adjustable.

We build the benchmark your problem actually needs, then measure against it honestly — task-specific evaluations, adversarial probes and regression harnesses wired into CI. Public benchmarks rarely measure the thing you are paid for, and a number that only moves on a leaderboard is a number that will not survive your first week in production. So we report the results that argue against the model we just trained for you, alongside the ones that flatter it.

06 — Surfaces

Grain, erosion, and colour that moves slowly.

Four fragment shaders, all fed the same three hues, all grained in the shader rather than by an overlay image. Grain is what stops a gradient reading as a 2014 startup header — it adds tooth, breaks the banding, and makes a flat colour feel like it was printed onto something. Each runs at low amplitude and very low speed; on a real page you would not consciously see them move.

Read them as candidates for the hero only. A shader behind body copy is a legibility problem wearing a nice coat — if one of these ships, it ships behind large display type with a solid panel for anything that has to be read.

07 — Components

The same three hues, doing actual work.

Swatches prove nothing. These are the shapes the palette has to survive: four button weights competing for the same eye, status pills that must be distinguishable at a glance, a table where the numbers matter more than the labels, and a stat block.

Buttons — one primary per view

Ink-900 is the default primary; indigo is for moments that are genuinely about the brand. Using both on one screen makes neither read as primary.

08 — Philosophy

Six rules, so the next decision argues with something.

A design system is mostly a record of arguments already settled. These are the ones worth settling first.

01

One curve, everywhere

Every transition on the site is 600ms on cubic-bezier(.22, 1, .36, 1), 24px of travel, fired once. The reason it reads as expensive is not that it is clever — it is that nothing deviates from it.

02

Colour needs a reason

Indigo is identity, teal is measurement, amber is caution. Any use that is not one of those three is decoration, and decoration is what makes a research lab look like a SaaS company.

03

Structure encodes something true

Numbered markers only where order matters. An eyebrow only where the section needs naming. A rule only where two things genuinely separate. Otherwise it is furniture.

04

Specific beats clever

"Cognitive architectures" told nobody anything. "Synthetic data generation" tells them whether to keep reading. Copy is design material, and vagueness is a visual problem as much as a verbal one.

05

Boldness gets spent once

A grained shader hero or a 96px italic serif headline — one of them, not both. Whatever the loud element is, everything within two screens of it goes quiet to pay for it.

06

Legibility outranks taste

If the calibration panel says a pairing lands under 4.5:1 at body size, it loses, regardless of how it looks in a swatch. This is the only rule here that is not negotiable.
